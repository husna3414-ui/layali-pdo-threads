After Your Treatment

Thread Lift Aftercare & Recovery

A calm, complete guide to your recovery — what's normal, what to avoid, and how to protect your beautiful lift while it settles.

Your PDO threads are now in place, lifting and supporting your tissue and beginning to stimulate fresh collagen. The first 1–2 weeks matter most — how you care for the area now directly protects your result.

A little swelling, bruising and a tight, pulling sensation are all completely normal in the early days. The first days are not your final result — trust the process, and never hesitate to reach out to us.

What is completely normal?

Normal & expected in the first 1–2 weeks

  • Swelling & bruising
  • Tenderness & soreness
  • A tightness or pulling sensation, especially when you move your face
  • Mild dimpling or puckering at entry points
  • Small bumps or areas of firmness under the skin
  • Feeling the threads slightly when you smile or talk
The pulling sensation is normal. It's a sign the threads are holding your lift, and it eases as healing progresses over the following weeks. Mild dimpling usually settles within the first 1–2 weeks.

Protect your lift — the first 1–2 weeks

Threads need time to settle and anchor. Big movements and pressure can disturb them before they've integrated, so please:

For ~1–2 weeks, avoid

  • Opening your mouth wide (big yawns, wide bites, dental appointments)
  • Exaggerated facial expressions — big laughs, wide smiles, frowning
  • Chewing tough or chewy foods — choose soft foods early on
  • Rubbing, pressing, massaging or touching the area
  • Sleeping on your side or face-down

Instead, please

  • Sleep on your back with your head elevated on 2–3 pillows
  • Keep facial movements gentle and small
  • Eat soft foods for the first few days
  • Support your face gently if you need to sneeze or yawn

What to avoid, and for how long

For ~2–4 weeks

  • Facials, facial massage & microcurrent
  • Microneedling, peels & other treatments on the area
  • Dental work
  • Deep pressure or manipulation of the area

For ~1–2 weeks

  • Heat — saunas, steam rooms & hot baths
  • Sunbeds & excessive sun
  • Strenuous exercise, gym & high-intensity workouts
  • Alcohol (especially the first few days)

Why: heat, pressure and vigorous movement increase swelling and can disturb the threads before they've settled.

Caring for your skin & entry points

Please do

  • Cleanse gently with a mild cleanser and pat dry
  • Keep the entry points clean & dry
  • Apply anything we've given you exactly as advised
  • Use SPF once healed to protect your skin

Please avoid

  • No makeup for 24 hours over the entry points
  • No scrubbing, exfoliating acids or retinol for now
  • No picking at entry points or any small scabs
  • Don't submerge in pools, baths or hot tubs early on

Keeping entry points clean and makeup-free for the first day lowers the risk of infection.

Managing discomfort

You may

  • Take paracetamol for soreness
  • Apply a clean cool compress gently (never ice directly on skin)
  • Use arnica gel/tablets to help bruising
  • Rest

Avoid (unless prescribed)

  • Ibuprofen & other anti-inflammatories
  • Aspirin
  • These can increase bruising and may interfere with early collagen response

If discomfort is severe or worsening rather than gradually easing, please contact us.

Please don't judge your result too early

In the first days, swelling can make the lift look higher or slightly uneven, and a little dimpling can appear. This is normal and settles. Your true lift is revealed once swelling resolves (around week 2), and firmness continues to improve as collagen builds over the following weeks. Patience is rewarded.

Warning signs — contact us straight away

Please contact Layali Clinic without delay if you notice:
  • A thread poking through the skin (extrusion) or a visible thread end
  • Spreading redness, heat, pus or discharge, or a fever (signs of infection)
  • Severe or worsening pain rather than gradual improvement
  • Marked or new asymmetry
  • Persistent, obvious dimpling or puckering that isn't settling
  • Any symptom that worries you
